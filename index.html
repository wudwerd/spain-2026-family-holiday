    Pals & the Empordà

    20 to 23 August / La Pallissa, Pals

    The first base sits between golden medieval villages and a huge dune-backed beach. Nobody sets an alarm at this end of the trip; the only fixture is lunch. The village of Pals itself is a short stroll from the door, best at the end of the day when the stone turns amber and the coaches have gone.

    La Pallissa

    Where we sleep for the Pals nights

    The old barn of a 1752 farmhouse, renovated top to bottom in 2020: stone walls, timber beams, a fireplace and a private garden with the fields behind. The lanes of the old town begin 350 metres from the door.

    Pals old town

    Golden stone, best at dusk

    Peratallada

    The prettiest village in the Empordà

    Carved out of a single outcrop of stone, moat and all. An hour covers it, go early, before the coaches and the midday crowd, then a long lunch in a courtyard while everyone quietly plans a life they are not going to lead here.

    Girona

    The city option

    Bridges over the Onyar, the Jewish Quarter, the cathedral steps and the best gelato of the trip. Don’t drive into the old town, park on the edge and walk in, and go early for the steps before the crowds. Easily cut short if the mood passes.

    Estany de Banyoles

    Fresh water under wooded hills

    Catalonia’s biggest lake, warm, swimmable in the marked zones. Fifty minutes inland, so it’s the full day out. A change from salt.

    Magma, Santa Coloma de Farners

    About 55 minutes inland: indoor and outdoor thermal pools, a counter-current river and a children’s area. Book a slot ahead, they sell out, and don’t expect a silent adult spa; it’s more kids’-play-park than serene, which for us is the point. Hold it for a grey day or when everyone’s tired of sand.

    The coves

    23 to 30 August / Camping Palafrugell

    A week with nothing booked. The coves below are all within half an hour, and nobody has to come to any of them. Every family has its own car, so every family has its own plan: one lot at the campsite pool, another snorkelling in Tamariu.

    Camping Palafrugell

    The campsite

    Timber cabins in the pine woods above Calella, porches and picnic tables out front. Clean and shady, and the beach is ten minutes downhill (and back up). Fair warning: the wifi’s hopeless, the beds are soft, and the pool has its daily sun-lounger wars. Home for the week.

    Nights
    23 to 30 August

    The Camí de Ronda

    The old coastguard path stitches Calella to Llafranc in twenty minutes of pines, coves and turquoise glimpses. Walk it before dinner when the light is honey and the day trippers have gone.

    Far de Sant Sebastià

    The lighthouse above Llafranc. Drive up, or slog up from the beach in half an hour, drink on the hotel terrace, look at the whole coast.

    Gardens over the sea

    How the days tend to go

    Decide after coffee.

    A few habits describe us. None of them tells anyone where they have to be.

    Move, if you want

    The early people

    Phil is the 05:30, 15 to 29 km runner. Estelle is more likely to head out around 08:00 for 5 km. Anyone else might train at about 08:30. Once they are back, the clock has finished its work.

    Beach, lightly

    Pick by mood

    Pool, cove, walk or nothing. A towel each, one small bag, children in the sea and lunch when everyone gets hungry.

    Children first

    Then the long table

    Feed the children at the campsite when it suits, then let them play within sight on the sand while the adults take their time over something delicious.

    Once or twice

    Stay in

    Spag bol, salad and cold bottles at the campsite. The rest of the time, follow the appetite and eat out.

    The local beach rule.

    A towel, a bag, the children. Done. No windbreak, tent, trolley, chair village or festival-grade encampment. Loïc's armbands come every time, but they are a swimming aid, not a substitute for an adult staying within arm's reach.
